# Lektion 03: Präsens — Regelmäßige Verben
## Present Tense — Regular Verbs

**Niveau:** A1
**Lernziele (Learning Objectives):**
- Understand the structure of German verb conjugation (stem + ending)
- Conjugate regular (weak) verbs in the present tense for all persons
- Handle special spelling cases: stems ending in -t/-d and -s/-ß/-z
- Apply the conjugation pattern to 15+ common regular verbs
- Build simple present tense sentences for everyday communication

---

## 1. Einführung (Introduction)

In the last lesson, you learned the irregular verbs "sein" and "haben." The good news
is that most German verbs follow a single, predictable pattern in the present tense.
Once you learn this pattern, you can conjugate hundreds of verbs correctly.

### Einführungsdialog (Introductory Dialogue)

> **Lisa:** Was machst du heute Abend?
> **Paul:** Ich koche Pasta. Und du?
> **Lisa:** Ich lerne Deutsch. Ich brauche gute Noten in der Prüfung.
> **Paul:** Wo lernst du? Zu Hause?
> **Lisa:** Nein, ich arbeite in der Bibliothek. Da ist es ruhig.
> **Paul:** Spielst du danach Fußball? Wir spielen um acht.
> **Lisa:** Vielleicht. Ich frage meine Freundin. Sie sucht auch eine Mannschaft.
> **Paul:** Super! Wir hören uns später. Tschüss!
> **Lisa:** Tschüss, Paul!

> **Lisa:** What are you doing this evening?
> **Paul:** I'm cooking pasta. And you?
> **Lisa:** I'm studying German. I need good grades on the exam.
> **Paul:** Where do you study? At home?
> **Lisa:** No, I work in the library. It's quiet there.
> **Paul:** Are you playing football afterwards? We play at eight.
> **Lisa:** Maybe. I'll ask my friend. She's also looking for a team.
> **Paul:** Great! We'll talk later. Bye!
> **Lisa:** Bye, Paul!

## 2. Grammatik-Erklärung (Grammar Explanation)

### 2.1 Das Grundprinzip (The Basic Principle)

Every German verb has an **infinitive** form that ends in **-en** (or rarely **-n**).
To conjugate, you:

1. **Remove the -en** to get the **stem** (Verbstamm)
2. **Add the personal ending** for each subject

**Formula:** STEM + ENDING = conjugated verb

**Example with "machen" (to make/do):**

```
machen → mach- (stem) + ending
```

### 2.2 Die Personalendungen (Personal Endings)

| Person | Pronomen | Endung (Ending) | Beispiel: machen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Person Singular | ich | **-e** | ich mach**e** |
| 2. Person Singular | du | **-st** | du mach**st** |
| 3. Person Singular | er/sie/es | **-t** | er mach**t** |
| 1. Person Plural | wir | **-en** | wir mach**en** |
| 2. Person Plural | ihr | **-t** | ihr mach**t** |
| 3. Person Plural | sie | **-en** | sie mach**en** |
| Formell | Sie | **-en** | Sie mach**en** |

> **Merke!** The endings for wir, sie (they), and Sie (formal) are always **-en** —
> which is identical to the infinitive! So for these three forms, the verb never changes.

> **Merke!** The endings for er/sie/es and ihr are both **-t**. The context (pronoun)
> makes it clear who the subject is.

### 2.4 Sonderfall 1: Verbstamm auf -t oder -d (Special Case: Stems Ending in -t or -d)

When the verb stem ends in **-t** or **-d** (or in **-m** or **-n** preceded by a
consonant other than l or r), an extra **-e-** is inserted before the endings **-st**
and **-t** for ease of pronunciation.

**Why?** Because "du arbeitst" would be nearly impossible to pronounce. So it becomes
"du arbeit**e**st."

#### arbeiten (to work) — Stamm: arbeit-

| Pronomen | Konjugation | Normal wäre... | Tatsächlich |
|---|---|---|---|
| ich | arbeit**e** | (regular) | arbeite |
| du | arbeit**est** | ~~arbeitst~~ | arbeitest |
| er/sie/es | arbeit**et** | ~~arbeitt~~ | arbeitet |
| wir | arbeit**en** | (regular) | arbeiten |
| ihr | arbeit**et** | ~~arbeitt~~ | arbeitet |
| sie/Sie | arbeit**en** | (regular) | arbeiten |

> **Merke!** The extra -e- appears only in the **du**, **er/sie/es**, and **ihr** forms.
> The ich, wir, sie/Sie forms are completely normal.

### 2.5 Sonderfall 2: Verbstamm auf -s, -ß, -z (Special Case: Stems Ending in -s, -ß, -z)

When the stem ends in **-s**, **-ß**, or **-z**, the "du" form ending loses the "s"
and becomes just **-t** (instead of -st). This is because the stem already ends in a
sibilant (hissing) sound.

| du | reis**t** | NOT "reisst" — the -s- is dropped |
| er/sie/es | reist | (regular) |
| wir | reisen | (regular) |
| ihr | reist | (regular) |
| sie/Sie | reisen | (regular) |

> **Merke!** For these verbs, the du-form and the er/sie/es-form look identical:
> du reist = er reist, du heißt = er heißt, du tanzt = er tanzt.

### 2.7 Das Präsens — Verwendung (Present Tense — Usage)

The German present tense (Präsens) is used more broadly than the English present tense:

| Verwendung (Use) | Deutsch | English |
|---|---|---|
| Habitual actions | Ich spiele jeden Tag Fußball. | I play football every day. |
| Current actions | Ich koche gerade Pasta. | I am cooking pasta right now. |
| General truths | Die Erde dreht sich um die Sonne. | The earth revolves around the sun. |
| Future (with time word) | Ich kaufe morgen ein Auto. | I will buy a car tomorrow. |
| Since (ongoing) | Ich wohne seit drei Jahren hier. | I have lived here for three years. |

> **Merke!** German has NO present progressive ("I am cooking"). "Ich koche" means
> both "I cook" AND "I am cooking." Context makes it clear.

> **Merke!** German often uses the present tense for future events when a time word
> (morgen, nächste Woche, etc.) makes the future meaning clear.

## 6. Zusammenfassung (Summary)

### Das Konjugationsmuster (The Conjugation Pattern):

```
Infinitiv (z.B. spielen) → Stamm (spiel-) + Endung (-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en)
```

### Endungen auf einen Blick (Endings at a Glance):

| | Normal | Stamm -t/-d | Stamm -s/-ß/-z |
|---|---|---|---|
| ich | -e | -e | -e |
| du | -st | -est | -t |
| er/sie/es | -t | -et | -t |
| wir | -en | -en | -en |
| ihr | -t | -et | -t |
| sie/Sie | -en | -en | -en |

### Wichtige Regeln (Key Rules):

- **Remove -en** from the infinitive to get the stem. Add the personal ending.
- **Stems ending in -t/-d** (arbeiten, reden, warten) insert an extra **-e-** before -st and -t endings: du arbeit**e**st, er arbeit**e**t, ihr arbeit**e**t.
- **Stems ending in -s/-ß/-z** (reisen, heißen, tanzen) drop the -s- in the du-form: du reis**t** (not reisst), du heiß**t**, du tanz**t**.
- **Stems ending in -m/-n after a consonant** (other than l, r) also take the extra -e-: du öffn**e**st, er rechn**e**t.
- The **wir/sie/Sie** forms always look the same as the infinitive.
- The **er/sie/es** and **ihr** forms always have the same ending (-t or -et).
- German present tense covers English present simple AND present progressive.
- German often uses present tense for future events with a time word.
