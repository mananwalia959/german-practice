# Lektion 01: Das Alphabet und die Aussprache
## The German Alphabet and Pronunciation

**Niveau:** A1
**Lernziele (Learning Objectives):**
- Learn all 26 letters of the German alphabet plus special characters
- Master the pronunciation of Umlauts (ä, ö, ü) and Eszett (ß)
- Understand and produce special German sound combinations (ch, sch, sp, st, z, w, v, j, qu, ei, ie, eu/äu)
- Recognize minimal pairs and practice distinguishing similar sounds
- Develop correct pronunciation habits from the very beginning

---

## 1. Einführung (Introduction)

German pronunciation is more consistent than English. Once you learn the rules, you can
read almost any German word aloud correctly — even if you have never seen it before.
Unlike English, German spelling is highly phonetic: letters and letter combinations almost
always produce the same sound.

### Begrüßungsdialog (Greeting Dialogue)

> **Anna:** Hallo! Ich heiße Anna. Wie heißt du?
> **Ben:** Hallo, Anna! Ich bin Ben. Ich komme aus Deutschland.
> **Anna:** Schön! Ich komme aus Österreich. Kannst du das buchstabieren?
> **Ben:** Klar! Ö-S-T-E-R-R-E-I-C-H.
> **Anna:** Perfekt! Deine Aussprache ist sehr gut.
> **Ben:** Danke! Übung macht den Meister!

> **Anna:** Hello! My name is Anna. What is your name?
> **Ben:** Hello, Anna! I am Ben. I come from Germany.
> **Anna:** Nice! I come from Austria. Can you spell that?
> **Ben:** Sure! Ö-S-T-E-R-R-E-I-C-H.
> **Anna:** Perfect! Your pronunciation is very good.
> **Ben:** Thanks! Practice makes perfect!

## 2. Grammatik-Erklärung (Grammar Explanation)

### 2.1 Das deutsche Alphabet (The German Alphabet)

German uses the same 26 Latin letters as English, plus four special characters.

| Buchstabe (Letter) | Name (German) | Approximate Sound |
|---|---|---|
| A a | ah | like "a" in "father" |
| B b | beh | like English "b"; at end of word, sounds like "p" |
| C c | tseh | like "ts" before e/i; like "k" before a/o/u |
| D d | deh | like English "d"; at end of word, sounds like "t" |
| E e | eh | like "e" in "bed" (short) or "ay" in "say" (long, without the glide) |
| F f | eff | like English "f" |
| G g | geh | like "g" in "go"; at end of word, can sound like "k" |
| H h | hah | like English "h" at start of syllable; silent after vowel (lengthens vowel) |
| I i | ih | like "ee" in "see" (long) or "i" in "sit" (short) |
| J j | yot | like "y" in "yes" |
| K k | kah | like English "k" |
| L l | ell | like English "l" |
| M m | emm | like English "m" |
| N n | enn | like English "n" |
| O o | oh | like "o" in "go" (long, without the glide) or "o" in "lot" (short) |
| P p | peh | like English "p" |
| Q q | kuh | always paired with "u"; "qu" = "kv" |
| R r | err | guttural/uvular "r" (back of throat); weakened at end of syllable |
| S s | ess | like "z" in "zoo" before vowels; like "s" in "sun" at end of word |
| T t | teh | like English "t" |
| U u | uh | like "oo" in "boot" (long) or "u" in "put" (short) |
| V v | fau | like "f" in most German words; like "v" in some foreign words |
| W w | weh | like English "v" in "vine" |
| X x | iks | like English "x" in "box" |
| Y y | üpsilon | like German "ü" |
| Z z | tsett | like "ts" in "cats" |

### 2.2 Sonderzeichen (Special Characters)

German has four characters not found in the standard English alphabet:

| Zeichen (Character) | Name | Approximate Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Ä ä | a-Umlaut | like "e" in "bed" or "ai" in "fair" |
| Ö ö | o-Umlaut | like "ur" in "burn" (lips rounded, say "eh") |
| Ü ü | u-Umlaut | like French "u" (lips rounded, say "ee") |
| ß | Eszett / scharfes S | like "ss" — a voiceless "s" |

> **Merke!** The Umlauts (ä, ö, ü) are NOT decorative — they are entirely different
> sounds from a, o, u. Mixing them up changes the meaning of words completely.

> **Achtung!** If you cannot type Umlauts, the accepted substitute is to add an "e"
> after the vowel: ä = ae, ö = oe, ü = ue, ß = ss. Example: München = Muenchen.

### 2.3 Die Umlaute im Detail (Umlauts in Detail)

#### Ä ä — The A-Umlaut

**How to produce it:** Open your mouth as if saying "ah," then shift the tongue
forward to the position for "eh." The result is a sound between "a" and "e."

| Short ä | Long ä |
|---|---|
| Sounds like "e" in "bet" | Sounds like "ai" in "fair" (sustained) |
| Äpfel (apples) | Käse (cheese) |
| Hände (hands) | spät (late) |
| Länder (countries) | Mädchen (girl) |
| kälter (colder) | wählen (to choose) |
| Männer (men) | erzählen (to tell) |

#### Ö ö — The O-Umlaut

**How to produce it:** Shape your lips as if saying "o" (rounded), but try to say "e."
Keep the lips rounded while the tongue is in the "e" position.

| Short ö | Long ö |
|---|---|
| Like a shortened "ur" in "burn" | Like a sustained "ur" in "burn" |
| öffnen (to open) | schön (beautiful) |
| Töpfe (pots) | hören (to hear) |
| Wörter (words) | mögen (to like) |
| können (can) | Löwe (lion) |
| Köln (Cologne) | böse (angry/evil) |

#### Ü ü — The U-Umlaut

**How to produce it:** Shape your lips as if saying "oo" (rounded), but try to say "ee."
Keep the lips tightly rounded while the tongue is in the "ee" position.

| Short ü | Long ü |
|---|---|
| Short, clipped version | Like French "u" in "rue" |
| fünf (five) | über (over/about) |
| Stück (piece) | Tür (door) |
| Glück (luck) | grün (green) |
| Brücke (bridge) | müde (tired) |
| dünn (thin) | Bücher (books) |

### 2.4 Das Eszett (ß)

The Eszett (ß) represents a voiceless "s" sound (like "ss"). It appears only after
long vowels and diphthongs.

| With ß | With ss |
|---|---|
| After a LONG vowel or diphthong | After a SHORT vowel |
| Straße (street) | Wasser (water) |
| groß (big/tall) | müssen (must) |
| heißen (to be called) | essen (to eat) |
| weiß (white/know) | lassen (to let) |
| Fuß (foot) | Schloss (castle) |
| Maß (measure) | Fass (barrel) |

> **Merke!** In Switzerland and Liechtenstein, ß is not used at all. They always write
> "ss" instead: Strasse, gross, heissen.

### 2.5 Besondere Buchstabenkombinationen (Special Letter Combinations)

#### CH — Two Different Sounds

| "Ich-Laut" (soft ch) | "Ach-Laut" (hard ch) |
|---|---|
| After e, i, ä, ö, ü, consonants | After a, o, u, au |
| Like a breathy "h" in "huge" | Like Scottish "ch" in "loch" |
| ich (I) | ach (oh) |
| Milch (milk) | Buch (book) |
| Kirche (church) | auch (also) |
| möchte (would like) | Nacht (night) |
| Bücher (books) | Kuchen (cake) |
| echt (real) | machen (to make) |
| Mädchen (girl) | Sprache (language) |
| Licht (light) | Tochter (daughter) |

> **Merke!** The "ch" at the beginning of a word (mostly foreign words) is pronounced
> like "k" (Charakter, Chor, Chaos) or like "sh" (Chef, Chance — borrowed from French).

#### SCH — Like English "SH"

|---|---|

| Schwester | sister |
| Deutsch | German |
| Tisch | table |
| Fisch | fish |
| Mensch | person/human |

| Geschenk | gift |

#### SP and ST at the Beginning of a Word/Syllable

When **sp** or **st** appear at the start of a word or syllable, they are pronounced
as **"shp"** and **"sht"**.

| Spelling | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| spielen | "shpeelen" | to play |
| Sport | "shport" | sport |
| sprechen | "shprekhen" | to speak |
| Spaß | "shpahss" | fun |
| Spinne | "shpinne" | spider |
| stehen | "shtehen" | to stand |
| Straße | "shtraasse" | street |
| Stuhl | "shtool" | chair |
| Stunde | "shtunde" | hour |
| verstehen | "fer-shtehen" | to understand |

> **Achtung!** This rule applies only at the BEGINNING of a word or syllable.
> In the middle or end of a word, sp and st keep their normal sounds:
> Wespe ("vespe," wasp), Fest ("fest," party/celebration).

### 2.6 Diphthonge (Diphthongs)

A diphthong is a combination of two vowels that form one sound.

#### EI / AI — Like English "eye"

| Wort | Bedeutung |
|---|---|
| eins | one |
| mein | my |
| klein | small |
| Eis | ice / ice cream |
| Reise | trip/journey |
| Arbeit | work |
| Freiheit | freedom |
| Mai | May |
| Kaiser | emperor |
| Bayern | Bavaria |

> **Merke!** EI always sounds like "eye." — Think of the word "Einstein."

#### IE — Like English "ee" in "see"

| Wort | Bedeutung |
|---|---|
| die | the (feminine) |
| sie | she/they |
| wie | how |
| Liebe | love |
| Brief | letter |
| Spiel | game |
| Bier | beer |
| Tier | animal |
| Lied | song |
| viel | much |

> **Achtung!** EI and IE are among the most commonly confused sounds for learners.
> Remember: EI = "eye" (the SECOND letter is heard), IE = "ee" (the SECOND letter is heard).
> Both times you hear the SECOND vowel! EI → you hear "I"; IE → you hear "E" (ee).

#### EU / ÄU — Like "oy" in "boy"

| Wort | Bedeutung |
|---|---|
| neu | new |
| deutsch | German |
| Freund | friend |
| heute | today |
| Leute | people |

| Europa | Europe |
| Häuser | houses |
| Bäume | trees |
| Träume | dreams |
| Verkäufer | salesperson |
| Gebäude | building |

> **Merke!** EU and ÄU are pronounced identically: both sound like "oy."
> ÄU is often the Umlaut plural of words with AU (Haus → Häuser, Baum → Bäume).

### 2.7 Lange und kurze Vokale (Long and Short Vowels)

German vowels can be either long or short, and this distinction changes meaning.

**Rules for LONG vowels:**
1. Before a single consonant: leben (to live), Hut (hat)
2. When doubled: Haar (hair), Beet (flower bed), Moor (moor)
3. Before an "h" (the h is silent): Uhr (clock), fahren (to drive), Bahn (train)
4. "ie" is always long: Liebe (love), Bier (beer)

**Rules for SHORT vowels:**
1. Before a double consonant: Bett (bed), Sonne (sun), Mutter (mother)
2. Before two or more different consonants: Hund (dog), Kinder (children)
3. In many one-syllable function words: in, mit, um, an, es

| Kurz (Short) | Bedeutung | Lang (Long) | Bedeutung |
|---|---|---|---|
| offen | open | Ofen | oven |
| Bett | bed | Beet | flower bed |
| Kamm | comb | kam | came |
| Lamm | lamb | lahm | lame |
| Hölle | hell | Höhle | cave |
| Hütte | hut/cabin | Hüte | hats |
| Mitte | middle | Miete | rent |
| Stadt | city | Staat | state |
| Wüste | desert | wüsste | would know |
| still | quiet | Stil | style |

### 2.8 Auslautverhärtung (Final Consonant Devoicing)

In German, voiced consonants (b, d, g) become voiceless (p, t, k) at the end of a
word or syllable. This is one of the most important pronunciation rules.

| Letter | End of word sounds like | Example | Pronounced as |
|---|---|---|---|
| b → p | p | Urlaub (vacation) | "Urlau**p**" |
| d → t | t | Hund (dog) | "Hun**t**" |
| g → k | k | Tag (day) | "Ta**k**" |
| b → p | p | halb (half) | "hal**p**" |
| d → t | t | Rad (wheel/bicycle) | "Ra**t**" |
| g → k | k | Zug (train) | "Tsu**k**" |

> **Merke!** The spelling does NOT change — only the pronunciation. You still write
> "Hund" but say "Hunt." You can hear the voiced sound again in longer forms:
> Hund → Hunde (dogs), Tag → Tage (days).

### 2.9 Zungenbrecher (Tongue Twisters)

Practice these classic German tongue twisters to improve your pronunciation:

1. **Fischers Fritz fischt frische Fische, frische Fische fischt Fischers Fritz.**
   (Fisher's Fritz fishes fresh fish, fresh fish fishes Fisher's Fritz.)

2. **Blaukraut bleibt Blaukraut und Brautkleid bleibt Brautkleid.**
   (Red cabbage remains red cabbage and wedding dress remains wedding dress.)

3. **Zwischen zwei Zwetschgenzweigen zwitschern zwei Schwalben.**
   (Between two plum branches, two swallows chirp.)

4. **In Ulm, um Ulm und um Ulm herum.**
   (In Ulm, around Ulm, and all around Ulm.)

5. **Wenn Schnecken an Schnecken schlecken, merken Schnecken zu ihrem Schrecken,
   dass Schnecken nicht schmecken.**
   (When snails lick snails, snails realize to their horror that snails don't taste good.)

6. **Der Cottbusser Postkutscher putzt den Cottbusser Postkutschkasten.**
   (The Cottbus stagecoach driver cleans the Cottbus stagecoach box.)

## 6. Zusammenfassung (Summary)

### Wichtige Regeln (Key Rules):

- **German spelling is phonetic** — learn the rules once, and you can pronounce any word.
- **Umlauts (ä, ö, ü)** are distinct sounds, not variations of a, o, u. They change word meaning.
- **ß (Eszett)** represents a voiceless "ss" and appears only after long vowels and diphthongs.
- **CH** has two sounds: soft "Ich-Laut" (after e, i, ä, ö, ü) and hard "Ach-Laut" (after a, o, u, au).
- **SCH** always sounds like English "sh."
- **SP** and **ST** at the beginning of a word or syllable are pronounced "shp" and "sht."
- **Z** is always "ts." **W** is always "v." **V** is usually "f." **J** is always "y."
- **QU** is pronounced "kv."
- **Diphthongs:** EI = "eye," IE = "ee," EU/ÄU = "oy," AU = "ow."
- **Long vs. short vowels** change word meaning (Stadt vs. Staat).
- **Final consonant devoicing:** b→p, d→t, g→k at end of word/syllable.
